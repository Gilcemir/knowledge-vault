How Google Sees MathFlow

Lesson 0001 · SEO for MathFlow · ~15 minutes · no prerequisites

SEO — search engine optimization — is the practice of making a website easy for search engines to find, understand, and recommend, so that people searching for what you offer actually reach you. No tricks involved: it's mostly about communicating clearly with a machine that reads your HTML.

Before touching a single tag, you need the one mental model every SEO decision hangs off: a search engine is a pipeline, and a page can fall out at any stage. By the end of this lesson you'll know the three stages, and you'll see exactly where MathFlow stands in each one today. Every technical term below is defined where it first appears — this course assumes zero SEO background.

The pipeline: crawl → index → serve

Google's own engineering docs describe Search as three stages, and — importantly — not every page makes it through each stage:

StageWhat happensHow a page fails here
1. Crawling Googlebot discovers URLs (via links and sitemaps) and downloads the page. If the page relies on JavaScript, Google also renders it with a headless Chromium. No links point to it, it's blocked by robots.txt, or the server errors.
2. Indexing Google analyzes the text, tags, images and decides what the page is about — and whether it's a duplicate of another page (canonicalization). A noindex tag, low-quality/duplicate content, or Google picks a different URL as canonical.
3. Serving For each query, Google ranks indexed pages by relevance and quality and renders a result: title link + snippet. The page is indexed but loses to more relevant or more trusted pages. This is where "ranking" lives.
Key idea Most beginners obsess over stage 3 (ranking) while their real problem is in stage 1 or 2. Diagnosing means asking, in order: Can Google reach it? Did Google index it? Why isn't it winning the query?

Where MathFlow stands today

I audited the repo (src/MathFlow/Pages/Shared/_Layout.cshtml and wwwroot/). Here's the honest picture, mapped to the pipeline:

Working in your favor

  • ✅ Server-rendered — Razor Pages sends complete HTML. No JavaScript-rendering risk in stage 1. This is a genuinely strong starting position that SPA developers have to fight for.
  • ✅ <html lang> set from the request culture.
  • ✅ Title pattern exists (@ViewData["Title"] - AppName), and headings are structured (one h1, then h2s).

Missing (each one is a future lesson + a real commit)

  • ❌ No meta description — a one-sentence summary you put in a <meta name="description"> tag; Google often shows it as the grey snippet text under your result. Without one, Google writes your snippet for you — badly. (stage 3: serving)
  • ❌ No robots.txt or sitemap — robots.txt is a file at your domain root that tells crawlers what not to fetch; a sitemap is an XML list of the URLs you want found. Both 404 on the live site today, and login/admin pages are crawlable. (stage 1: crawling)
  • ❌ No canonical URLs — a <link rel="canonical"> tag declaring "this is the official address of this page", so /converter, /Converter and /converter?x=1 don't get indexed as competing duplicates. (stage 2: indexing)
  • ❌ No hreflang — tags that tell Google "this page exists in English, Portuguese, and Spanish; here are the siblings". Your site is localized EN/pt-BR/es but never says so, so Google may show Spanish searchers the English page. (stage 3: serving the right language — and your priority audience is PT-BR/ES)
  • ❌ No structured data — a small JSON block (called JSON-LD) describing the page in a machine vocabulary; a free web tool is exactly what the WebApplication type is for, and it can earn a richer-looking result. (stage 3)
  • ⚠️ Homepage title renders as "OMML2MathML - OMML2MathML" — the page sets the brand as its title and the layout appends the brand again. Beyond the duplication, nobody searches that string; titles should speak the searcher's language ("Convert Word equations to MathML"). (stage 3)
Gotcha robots.txt does not hide a page from Google — it only stops crawling. A blocked-but-linked page can still be indexed (URL only). To keep login/admin pages out of results, the tool is noindex, not robots.txt. Google's robots.txt intro is explicit about this — it's the most common beginner mixup.

Try it now — 5 minutes, real feedback

Hands-on
  1. Search Google for site:omml2mathml.com. This operator lists every URL Google has indexed (stage 2) for your domain. Count them. Any pages there that shouldn't be (login? register?)? Any missing (the converter itself)?
  2. Pick any site you admire and view its source (Ctrl/Cmd+U). Find, in the <head>: the <title>, <meta name="description">, <link rel="canonical">, and any <script type="application/ld+json">. These four are the skeleton MathFlow is missing.
Report back what site: found — it decides whether our next lesson is about getting indexed at all, or about improving what's already indexed.

Check yourself

MathFlow's converter page is live and linked from the homepage, but it never appears for any query — not even site: searches. Which stage is the problem most likely in?

Absent from site: means Google has not indexed the URL — a stage 1/2 problem. Ranking (stage 3) only matters for pages that are already in the index. First check: URL Inspection in Search Console.

You want MathFlow's /admin pages to never show up in Google results. What's the correct mechanism?

robots.txt only stops crawling — a linked URL can still be indexed without content. Sitemaps are discovery hints, not exclusion. Only noindex (meta tag or X-Robots-Tag header) removes a page from results — and the page must be crawlable for Google to see it.
