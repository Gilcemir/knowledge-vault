Search Console: Find the Missing Pages

Lesson 0002 · SEO for MathFlow · ~15 min reading + ~15 min hands-on · builds on Lesson 1

Your site:omml2mathml.com search found one indexed page — the homepage. The converter itself, the page your whole site exists for, is invisible to Google. Before fixing anything we need to know why, and guessing is not diagnosing. This lesson gives you the instrument panel: Google Search Console.

Key idea A site: search is a quick approximation — it shows roughly what's indexed, but not why pages are missing. Search Console is the ground truth: Google's free dashboard that tells site owners exactly how Google crawls, indexes, and serves their site, page by page and query by query. Every indexing question from now on gets answered there, not by guessing.

What Search Console gives you

URL Inspection"What does Google know about this exact URL?" — crawled? indexed? when? why not?1 & 2

One vocabulary pair you'll see constantly in the Performance report: an impression is your page being shown in someone's results; a click is them choosing it. Impressions without clicks usually means a weak title/snippet — which is exactly MathFlow's current situation.

Proving you own the site

Google won't show this data to strangers, so step one is verification — proving omml2mathml.com is yours. Two property types exist (official guide):

  • Domain property (omml2mathml.com) — covers every subdomain and both http/https. Verified with a DNS TXT record at your domain registrar. Recommended — you own the domain, and one verification covers everything forever.
  • URL-prefix property (https://omml2mathml.com/) — verified by an HTML file upload or a meta tag. Simpler if DNS access is awkward, but covers only that exact prefix.
Gotcha DNS changes can take minutes to a day to propagate. If verification fails right after you add the TXT record, don't undo anything — wait and hit "Verify" again later. The record must stay in your DNS permanently, or verification silently lapses.

Why might /converter be missing? (hypotheses to test, not conclusions)

From Lesson 1's audit, the suspects — in the order URL Inspection will let you check them:

  1. Google never discovered it well. There's no sitemap, so Google only finds pages by following links. The homepage does link to /converter… but discovery ≠ indexing.
  2. Discovered but not crawled/indexed yet. New, small sites with few external links get a small crawl budget and Google can be slow and picky. Very common; the report literally has a category "Discovered — currently not indexed".
  3. Something blocks or demotes it — an accidental noindex, a redirect to login, an error for Googlebot. Less likely given the repo audit, but this is why we inspect instead of assume.

Try it now — this week's 15 applied minutes

Hands-on
  1. Go to search.google.com/search-console and add a Domain property for omml2mathml.com. Copy the TXT record it gives you into your DNS (wherever the domain is registered), then verify.
  2. Once in, open URL Inspection (top search bar) and inspect https://omml2mathml.com/converter. Read the verdict: is it "URL is not on Google"? What reason does it give under "Page indexing"?
  3. If it says the page is crawlable and there's no blocker, click Request Indexing — a legitimate nudge for individual URLs.
  4. Check the Pages report and note what each non-indexed URL is excluded for.
Bring the URL Inspection verdict for /converter to the next session — it picks our fix: sitemap first, internal linking, or removing a blocker.

Check yourself

Search Console's Performance report shows a query got 400 impressions and 2 clicks for your page. What does that most directly tell you?

Impressions mean Google is already showing the page (stages 1–2 are fine, and stage 3 at least surfaces it). People seeing it and not clicking points at the title and snippet — the page's "ad copy" in the results. Indexing failure would mean zero impressions.

URL Inspection says /converter is "Discovered — currently not indexed". What does that mean?

"Discovered — currently not indexed" means Google has the URL in its to-do list but hasn't fetched (or has fetched and deferred) it — typical for small new sites. A noindex or a fetch error would each be named explicitly as different statuses. The usual remedies: sitemap, stronger internal links, and patience.
